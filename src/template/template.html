THE BRIEFING: From Language to Physical Intelligence

Good morning. It is Thursday, May 15, 2026, coinciding with the International Day of Families. While we celebrate the social structures that bind us, the technology world is focused on the physical structures that define our existence. Today marks a definitive shift in the AI narrative: we are graduating from the era of chatbots to the era of World Foundation Models (WFMs).

The industry is moving away from purely text-based systems toward grounded intelligence. This evolution is spearheaded by the predictive world modeling research of Yann LeCun at AMI Labs and the spatial intelligence breakthroughs of Fei-Fei Li at World Labs. The goal is no longer just to make a machine that talks like a human, but one that perceives and reasons within a 3D environment just as we do.

CORE ARCHITECTURE: From Generation to Prediction

A key research direction in this space is Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). Unlike traditional generative models that try to reconstruct every pixel (a process that is computationally wasteful and prone to hallucinating physical laws), JEPA learns by predicting the relationship between objects in an abstract latent space.

Yann LeCun's Contribution: The Architect of Common Sense

Yann LeCun has long argued that LLMs lack the common sense of a house cat. His fundamental contribution is the Objective Driven AI framework, realized through V-JEPA (Video Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture).

1) Non-Generative Prediction: Instead of generating video pixels, LeCun's model predicts abstract representations. This allows the AI to ignore noise (like shifting shadows) and focus on signal (the path of a moving object).

2) Predictive World Modeling: Following his departure from Meta, LeCun's new venture, AMI Labs, announced a $1.03 billion seed round on March 10, 2026. It is Europe's largest seed round on record, valuing the company at $3.5 billion pre-money. His goal is to enable machines to internalize intuitive physics: the unspoken rules of gravity, friction, and object permanence.

SPATIAL INTELLIGENCE: Seeing the 4D World

While LeCun builds the physics reasoning framework, Fei-Fei Li is building the spatial imagination for the next generation of AI.

Fei-Fei Li's Contribution: From ImageNet to World Labs

Dr. Fei-Fei Li's newest frontier is spatial intelligence. Through her venture World Labs, which raised $1 billion in new funding on February 18, 2026 from backers including NVIDIA, AMD, Autodesk, Fidelity, and Andreessen Horowitz, she is moving AI from 2D recognition to 4D understanding. (Bloomberg reported in January 2026 that funding talks were occurring at approximately a $5 billion valuation, though the final valuation was not formally disclosed.)

1) Large World Models: Li's team has pioneered models that go beyond video, creating persistent, navigable 3D environments. Unlike a static video, a World Labs-generated environment is a digital twin that can be explored from any angle.

2) Embodied AI: Li argues that human intelligence is not just linguistic. Her work bridges the gap between vision and robotics, ensuring that machines can not only describe a room but understand its geometry and layout well enough to navigate it safely.

BUILDING FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES: Suryansh's Machine Learning Course

Not every learning experience happens in a classroom. Sometimes it happens when a student, unsatisfied with the surface-level treatment of a subject, decides to build something deeper from scratch, for everyone. That is exactly what Suryansh, a member of the AIRIS community, has been doing.

Suryansh launched a peer-led Machine Learning course designed around a single, deliberate philosophy: understand concepts from first principles rather than just learning how to use existing tools and libraries. While the university's Introduction to Generative AI course sparked his initial interest in the field, he found it did not go deep enough into the mathematical foundations that make these systems actually work.

What the Course Covers

What does it mean to learn?

Starting from the core ML principle of minimising loss by tuning model parameters, derived manually rather than lifted from a textbook.

Gradient Descent: Unboxed

Gradients derived from scratch, including a deep look at why squared loss is preferred over absolute loss beyond just "it's not differentiable."

Regression & Classification

Linear Regression, Logistic Regression: both the mathematics and the implementation, connected end-to-end.

Tensor Calculus

Introductory tensor calculus bridging the gap between mathematical theory and modern ML frameworks.

The Teaching Philosophy: Socratic Learning

After experimenting with different approaches, Suryansh gradually settled on a Socratic method: asking targeted questions that guide students toward discovering answers themselves, rather than simply delivering answers. The first lecture went largely as planned. The ones that followed became denser as he tried to stick to a rigid schedule. Eventually, he let go of the structure and allowed each session to breathe. If a topic was too heavy for one sitting, it would continue the following week.

An Honest Reflection

There were many difficult moments along the way. Sometimes I was asked questions I could not answer immediately, which made me question whether I was even qualified to teach. Some days I stuttered; other days I could clearly feel that I had failed to communicate an idea effectively. But I kept going.

In the end, I realized that I am doing this primarily for myself, to become better at thinking, understanding, and explaining. The ideal scenario is when my personal goals align with everyone else's learning goals, but that is not always possible.

The course started with around 30 students. About 7 remain as regular participants. He plans to continue.

At its core, Suryansh's course is a reminder that the deepest learning often happens when you decide to teach. And that within AIRIS, members are not just consumers of knowledge, but builders of it.
 

AIRIS NEWS

The AIRIS Club continues to build a strong culture of innovation, research, and practical AI learning through impactful projects, technical sessions, and industry exposure. Here are some upcoming events and projects.

Projects & Initiatives

1) RTW Prediction Model

Category: Open-Source Development Marking a milestone as the first open-source project under the AIRIS GitHub organization, the RTW (Return-to-Work) Prediction Model utilizes real workers' compensation claims data to forecast employment outcomes. The project is strategically divided into classification and regression tracks, serving as a primary vehicle for teaching production-level workflows. Members gain vital experience in GitHub collaboration, branching strategies, and CI/CD basics, establishing a high standard for teamwork and open-source contribution within the club.

2) Divuze – AI-Based Virtual Try-On Startup Project

Category: Startup & Innovation Divuze is an entrepreneurial venture focused on revolutionizing the e-commerce experience through an AI-powered virtual try-on platform. By allowing users to visualize clothing on their own bodies digitally, the project addresses critical consumer pain points like fit uncertainty and styling confidence. This initiative serves as a bridge between AI technology and business thinking, giving members hands-on exposure to product design, prototyping, and the foundational elements of building a tech startup.

Events & Sessions

1) Participation in AI Impact Summit

On February 18–19, 2026, 27 members of AIRIS attended a high-profile two-day summit centered on innovation and emerging technologies. This engagement provided members with critical exposure to the national AI landscape and the latest advancements in entrepreneurship. Beyond gaining career insights from industry experts and startup leaders, the delegation successfully networked with various companies and universities, significantly strengthening the club's external professional presence.

2) Session on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by Jivit Rana

Led by Jivit Rana, this session introduced members to Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), a cornerstone of modern generative AI. The workshop focused on the practical implementation of RAG systems, demonstrating how Large Language Models (LLMs) can be optimized using external knowledge retrieval. By bridging the gap between theory and industry-standard workflows, the session equipped participants with the tools necessary for high-level research and application development.

3) Kaggle Competition Initiative

AIRIS organized the event to promote hands-on learning in data science through real-world competitive problem-solving. Members were introduced to how Kaggle competitions operate within the professional data science ecosystem while exploring different models, feature engineering techniques, and score optimization strategies. The experience encouraged analytical thinking, experimentation, and continuous improvement, while fostering a competitive yet collaborative learning environment within AIRIS.

CONCLUSION

Artificial intelligence is no longer confined to answering questions. It is beginning to understand the world in which those questions arise. From LeCun's JEPA-based world models to Li's spatially-intelligent environments, the field is making a deliberate shift toward grounded, physically-aware reasoning. At THE AIRIS CHRONICLE, we will continue tracking these developments and making sure you stay informed every step of the way.
